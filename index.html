What the data is saying so far

Only 4 in 10 officers filled out the retreat survey, and under 3% of Tate Time attendees. The plan aims for 7 in 10. Getting the form into the room — a QR on every table, five minutes held back at the close — is the cheapest fix available, and everything else here gets more reliable when it lands.

Succession keeps surfacing. Presidents rate their vision for the year at 5 out of 5 and their plan for the leaders who follow them at 3.7. The same gap appeared at the Council launch. Three separate surveys are pointing at it.

Money is the biggest open question. Five of fourteen presidents named budgets or allocations; all three organizations reporting so far asked for financial or fundraising support. September’s Executive Forum can answer it directly.

Nearly half the officers who commented asked for more hands-on activity and less lecture — a change we can make to the spring retreat now.

Two entries is a start, not a picture

The service-use rate is the measure OO1 turns on — the share of affiliated organizations receiving at least one documented support interaction, against a goal of 75%. Right now that reads 1 of 34.

That almost certainly understates what is happening. Advising conversations are taking place; they are not being logged. Until they are, the office cannot show the support it is providing, and cannot identify which organizations are getting none.

Both entries so far were under fifteen minutes, and one already needed handing to another staff member — which is exactly the pattern the log exists to surface.

Why these matter more than they look

These two debriefs are the only place the office has an attendance figure for either programme. Every response rate on this dashboard depends on someone writing that number down within a couple of days of the event.

They also caught something no participant survey would: Silent Disco drew 109 people and the staff view was that engagement was strong but attendance fell short of expectation — with marketing named as the fix. That is a finding about promotion, not programming, and it only exists because someone wrote it down.

Early days, but two things already stand out

Four of the five came specifically for Tate Time rather than finding it while passing through. That is not what you would expect of a drop-in in one of the busiest buildings on campus, and it suggests the programme has a following rather than an audience of passers-by.

Two of the five belong to no affiliated organization at all — students the office is reaching who are not already inside a CSO. That is the number worth watching as more sessions are surveyed.

Five responses is far too few to lean on. The staff debrief records 110 people at the 26 August session alone, and three of them responded — under 3%. The attendee emails have not gone out yet, and that is the single thing that would change it.
